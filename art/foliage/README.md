# Tree foliage source textures

The oak, ash, willow, maple and teak `*-spray-source.png` files and `pine-spray-v2.png` were generated with the built-in image generation tool for Corealm. These are synthetic foliage images. Walnut and yew now have dedicated compound-leaf and flat-needle sprays. Red maples use `maple-red-spray-source.png`; magic trees use their own blue-green, silver-veined spray with flowing surface particles along the bark and a separate shimmering leaf canopy. The replacement sprays and bark scan were also generated with the built-in image generation tool. The reference collages guided tree structure and were not copied into the textures. See [generation prompts](./generation-prompts.md).

The source briefs requested isolated branch sprays with stems at the bottom center, transparent backgrounds, natural internal gaps, diffuse green color, and no directional shadows. Each selected texture was visually inspected before use.

`tools/build-corealm-nature.ts` resamples each source to a 1024 × 1024 RGBA texture and embeds it in the corresponding tree GLBs. Eight-triangle curved cards carry complete branch sprays. Alpha testing writes depth and uses the same silhouette in wind and shadow passes.

The embedded PNGs retain ordinary straight RGBA for asset portability. At load time, the renderer prepares a shared texture with RGB weighted by alpha in linear space. GPU filtering then averages leaf colour without introducing the black RGB behind transparent pixels. The leaf shader recovers that colour after filtering and uses a slightly wider mip footprint for colour only. Alpha bytes, geometry and the shadow cutoff stay intact. Both visible sides of a branch spray share the same upward lighting normal, and the sun's shadow grid stays anchored as the camera moves. `tools/foliage-motion-lab-test.ts` captures production camera and wind sequences for reviewing temporal stability.

Broadleaf geometry uses continuous wood axes with smaller laterals at separate attachment points. It replaces the space-colonization pass that repeatedly produced similar forks. Mature oak and magic trees lose their straight upper leader; walnut retains a higher clear bole, willow supports hanging fine shoots on arching limbs, and yew keeps low horizontal branches. Pine retains its central leader and uneven needle-bearing boughs. See [tree construction research and variant briefs](./tree-construction.md) for the botanical references, original modeling research and the distinction between sourced traits and art direction. Geometry is generated offline; the game loads and instances 24 models.

Regenerate candidates with `npx tsx tools/build-corealm-nature.ts --stage test-results/natural-trees/candidate` and inspect them in the production environment workbench before replacing game assets. After acceptance, run `npx tsx tools/build-corealm-nature.ts`, then `npx tsx tools/publish-corealm-trees.ts`. The publisher verifies all tree file hashes and updates only tree rows and the nature generator hash in the existing manifest.

The 24 tree models range from 8,794 to 38,576 triangles each. Mature oak and walnut have broader crowns and heavier trunks. Each species has its own foliage texture and crown dimensions; the variants change age, lean, crown spread and branch survival. Opposite foliage is placed on fine ash, maple and teak shoots. These are source geometry counts, not frame-rate measurements.

Species progression is Pine 1, Ash 5, Oak 10, Walnut 20, Willow 30, Maple 40, Teak 50, Yew 60, and Magic 70. Regional tiers provide a preference within mixed forests. Lower and higher tiers share the remaining pool; future tiers decline gradually with the level gap. Cluster dominance is capped at 35%. Willows use dry shoreline bands rather than field scatter.

Botanical references: [oak](https://plants.ces.ncsu.edu/plants/quercus-robur/common-name/common-oak/), [walnut](https://plants.ces.ncsu.edu/plants/juglans-nigra/), [willow](https://plants.ces.ncsu.edu/plants/salix-babylonica/), [yew](https://plants.ces.ncsu.edu/plants/taxus-baccata/), and [teak](https://www.nparks.gov.sg/florafaunaweb/flora/3/1/3178). The models interpret these habits for the game's art style; magic is a fantasy species.

Acceptance uses `tools/foliage-distance-lab-test.ts` for all 24 trees and four understory models, `tools/forest-handoff-lab-test.ts` for continuous visibility while walking, and `tools/forest-lab-test.ts` for gathering and persistence. Each accepts `--url http://127.0.0.1:4188`. Screenshots and reports go to ignored `test-results/` directories. Visual reviews include single species, near/far views and a mixed grove.

World scatter composition and coastal resource registration use the world-authoring exception to the isolated lab gate: biome mixing and the playable land outside semantic rectangles require the authored island. Assets, materials and interactions use the lab first. Living coastal trees now inherit the nearest semantic region instead of being left as unclickable decoration. Final integration samples loaded world tiles and real tree interactions with `tools/world-resource-check.ts --forest-only`; this is not a full island census.


Short overlapping sprays follow secondary limbs and their fine shoots. Their position, size and angle vary along that growth; terminals do not all receive the same star-shaped fan. Magic has a heavy divided stem and an independent leaf atlas. `bark-ridges-source.png` is embedded as a 1024 px JPEG, with bump relief using the same registered texture and UVs. The renderer preserves this authored bark rather than replacing it with the legacy normalized surface.


Wood grows through unequal extensions with local changes of heading. Thick limbs round each turn across adjoining growth sections; fine shoots have more freedom to bend. The clear lower bole has a mild lean, with pronounced changes of heading above the first scaffold limbs. Shoot spacing, survival, length and direction vary along the local parent limb. The main stems use separate growth sections instead of a single polynomial bow. Seeded species and age choices mix single leaders with two substantial daughter stems; the older oak and magic forms can divide again higher up. Each daughter carries its own crown branches. Young oaks and pines retain a stronger single leader.

The wood sweep bounds its tangents between unequal sections, uses surface normals from adjoining faces, and retains creases at tight joints. These changes prevent folded collars and incorrect lighting where growth turns. Magic's particles use bark arc-length UVs to flow along angled limbs; their cores, halos and short tails move at varied speeds. Leaf shimmer uses a separate travelling colour wave and vein glints. The production clock drives both effects without moving collision geometry.

At a main-stem division, the trunk and one daughter share a continuous wood sweep. A shared full-width ring tapers into the daughter's diameter over four parent radii. The other daughter has a flared collar. This removes the exposed flat cap and abrupt shoulder that separate cylinders left at the fork, including repeated upper divisions. Red maple cutouts preserve their source saturation through the organic material pass instead of receiving the generic foliage desaturation. All species retain their authored materials when promoted to interactive trees. Previously only oak and pine bypassed the mineral tier palette, so nearby maples received the level-40 ice-blue tint and emission. Tree recognition now uses the species registry for materials, wind, cache keys and stump handling.

The environment workbench offers a **Wood only** checkbox backed by `setFoliageWoodOnly(boolean)`. It hides the leaf meshes of the existing production fixture and leaves its wood in place. The branch-growth review inspected all 24 variants fully leafed and without leaves. Staged evidence is in `test-results/branch-growth/review/`. The forest gathering gate accepts `--catalog <candidate.json>` to exercise unpublished models through real canvas input before promotion.

The later fork-transition review repeats all 24 leafed and wood-only views, adds opposing close views of the oak junction, and checks both maples at 38 m. Its disposable screenshots and reports are in `test-results/fork-transition/`. The topology guard checks that each main continuation shares one full-width ring with its parent and ends beyond the fork, so a capped shoulder cannot return unnoticed.

The interaction-colour review in that directory compares the same maple camera before and after forest promotion (`palette/`), walks the eastern lane through 1,015 rendered frames with no missing or duplicate trees (`handoff/`), and verifies live maple and oak views in Vellenwood (`world/`). The production forest gathering and persistence gate also passes with the expanded 20-tree fixture. `tests/tree-entity-materials.test.ts` checks the production entity material path for all 24 tree variants and both shared stumps across species tiers.

The subsequent split-departure adjustment makes each substantial daughter follow the parent heading through a short collar, capped at 1.6 parent radii before rounding into the first extension. Later growth sections retain their changes of direction. The nine affected divided variants have leafed and wood-only lab views in `test-results/split-departure/review/`, with opposing oak close-ups and a magic junction in `after/`. The topology guard checks that departure stays within 12 degrees of the parent while widely spreading stems still diverge beyond the collar.
