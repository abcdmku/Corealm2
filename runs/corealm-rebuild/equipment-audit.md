# Equipment and item presentation audit

The largest proven gaps are lost material detail, mismatched inventory art, and missing presentation for the highest elemental tier. The equipment catalogue is mapped, but mapped does not mean polished. Common weapons and armour are authored imported meshes; the evidence does not support replacing every model with new geometry before fixing the importer and material path.

This was a read-only source, shipped-asset metadata, and existing-image review on 2026-09-04. Only this report was written. No browser, build, tests, combined lab gate, or whole-game checks ran. Uncommitted implementation work was preserved. References describe the checked working tree, including its current local changes.

## Coverage

The production registry contains 195 items: 95 equipment, 15 tools, 22 resources, 4 bars, 42 components, 16 food, and 1 currency. The appearance catalogue was read through its production module. The current 48 px contact sheet was visually inspected across all 195 entries. That establishes icon coverage and broad consistency, not individual worn-model acceptance.

## Bounded changes, in priority order

1. Restore common-equipment material response at import, then assess the actual meshes under production lighting. `tools/build-assets.ts:1573` deletes normal, metallic/roughness, occlusion and emissive maps and forces metalness to zero. The source header at `:12` also declares a universal 512 px base-colour-only policy. The shipped weapon and armour GLBs confirm its effect. Add an equipment-specific retention policy for authored maps; retain lossless normal data and suitable resolution for close inspection. Limit this first change to common weapon and modular outfit picks at `:623` and `:731`. Preserve UVs, skeletons, pivots, item IDs, and socket contracts. Verify plate, leather, cloth and wood separately. A generic noise overlay or uniform metalness increase cannot replace separate material zones.

2. Preserve wood detail on magic weapons. `game/src/render/equipmentVisuals.ts:701` sets the equipped Blink model's `map` to null, clears emissive maps and vertex colours, and forces metalness 0.04 / roughness 0.72. This makes ten plain weapons and eight elemental bases share one solid colour treatment despite shipping a 2048 px albedo. Keep starter and uncharged weapons unlit while rebuilding their wood colour from a texture or mask that retains grain, grip wear and crown construction. Use the existing normal map. Keep the current grip pivot and exterior dimensions during this material-only round. Historical `runs/corealm/screenshots/magic-visual-basic-staff-unlit.png` and `magic-wood-cairnpine-wand-unlit.png` show the broad uniform colour and long rod silhouette; they do not prove the present animation fit.

3. Complete Fire weapon charge presentation and replace the coarse socket. `game/src/render/equipmentVisuals.ts:488` has Air, Earth and Water palette entries but no `fire_wand` or `fire_staff`. `:520` returns the plain model when the palette is absent, even though released Fire weapons and charge metadata exist at `game/src/content/equipment.ts:797` and `:814`. Add both Fire mappings. At `equipmentVisuals.ts:766`, the visible core is a 20-triangle untextured icosahedron. Replace that shared shape with a finished crystal/orb appropriate to the crown, with a shaped socket contact and a material that reads charged versus depleted. Keep the documented socket positions until lab framing proves any adjustment. Add narrow coverage for all eight charged/depleted variants; current coverage at `tests/equipment.test.ts:226` checks only charged Air Wand and depleted Water Staff.

4. Make generated icons retain the finished item's material and identity. `game/src/render/itemIconRenderer.ts:534` clears the albedo and vertex colours on every tinted imported item, and `:536` constrains material response. The current contact sheet confirms uniformly coloured armour, shields and rare weapons. Preserve authored material zones, wear and texture information through a controlled exposure/tint treatment, then tune individual item framing. `:542` also reduces every rare emissive accent to 0.15 instead of matching the authored-map-aware equipped treatment at `equipmentVisuals.ts:691`. Keep the fixed transparent image dimensions and existing UI URL contract. Review masters and actual 48 px results; large detailed renders alone do not settle inventory readability.

5. Give elemental icons a visible core and synchronize dagger artwork. `game/src/render/itemIconAppearances.ts:279` routes every non-rare magic weapon directly to the plain staff/wand asset and never includes its elemental socket. Existing masters are byte-identical for Palewood Staff / Air Staff, Cinderpine Staff / Fire Staff, and Palewood Wand / Air Wand, confirmed by SHA-256 reads. Add an item-identity socket to elemental icon appearances. At `:284`, all daggers use an unrelated broad procedural blade; worn daggers are uniformly shrunken swords at `equipmentVisuals.ts:190`. Author one finished dagger model with a full-size grip and a short blade, then reuse it for worn and icon views. Measure the new socket instead of inheriting the sword offset.

6. Fix hand-item icon staging. `art/item-icons/256/grithe_gloves.png` visibly contains two widely separated forearms in their bind pose. At 48 px they reduce to two thin diagonal fragments on the contact sheet. `itemIconRenderer.ts:563` clones the outfit skeleton without posing it, then fits the full pair's bounds. Pose or stage gloves as an overlapping pair around a useful palm/back view, and frame their glove/cuff region. Apply the same treatment to all four Knight and four Ranger hand items. Use authored fingers and cuff detail rather than replacing them with a generic mitten icon. The same contact-sheet review should catch excessively narrow sword, staff and rod icons after the material pass.

7. Finish fishing-tool coverage and its production shape. `game/src/render/proceduralGear.ts:246` defines only Worn, Palewood, Duskoak and Cairnpine rods. The catalogue and icons also include Cinderpine. `:325` selects Cairnpine for every tier at least 10; `game/src/render/characterRig.ts:852` falls back through that table when the actual Cinderpine item is absent. Add the real Cinderpine appearance. The current rod at `proceduralGear.ts:458` is three straight shaft sections with a baked three-section line and bobber; all parts share one untextured material at `:693`. Build a continuous tapered rod, finished grip/bindings, and a production line that responds to the fishing target and motion. Its icon currently invents a large reel at `itemIconRenderer.ts:331` that the held mesh does not have. Reuse the finished production tool in the icon pipeline. Do not spend this round polishing the obsolete procedural staff/wand registrations: current equipment resolves the imported Blink assets instead.

8. Make equipment fit inspection cover body variant, headwear and hand use. `game/src/render/characterRig.ts:920` calls the body-aware appearance functions without forwarding the rig's body variant, so they default to male. `:568` already chooses the correct body for preloading. Forward body selection consistently through the render port and its callers; root owns that contract change. Hair remains a separate region at `:247` and `:258` when headwear is installed. Existing `test-results/feature-labs/combat-melee.png` shows a pale shape protruding above the helmet, but its reduced graphics are insufficient to decide whether this is hair penetration or intentional geometry. Add close front/side/rear headwear inspection, then hide or trim only hair covered by each helmet/hood. The animation table at `:133` uses the same idle/walk/cast choices for wand and two-handed staff, with no support-hand constraint. A held staff in the historical screenshot is carried in one hand despite two-handed gameplay metadata. Add staff-specific support-hand poses where contact is required. Preserve current measured primary-hand sockets and verify them through idle, run, cast, melee, mining and woodcutting samples instead of moving all weapons together.

9. Give tier families more than a whole-object recolour where an upgrade must read visually. `equipmentVisuals.ts:186` assigns all four melee tiers the same Knight shapes and all four magic tiers the same Ranger shapes. `:362` gives even the first melee chest the same pauldron and scarf assembly. Rare regional weapons share two shapes at `:288`. Current source descriptions promise differences such as an open-faced Grithe cap, Corven sight slit and Kaldite brow ridge, but the mapping returns the same helmet for all three. First add regional material masks with distinct cloth, leather, metal and stone treatment; then make bounded silhouette changes to helmet opening, plate edges, cuffs, crown and rare-weapon motifs without changing rig proportions. A uniform scale increase enlarges the grip and cannot express construction quality. Treat model replacement as a per-family decision after current material detail is restored.

10. Replace the most misleading non-equipment icon proxies, then finish their surfaces. `game/src/render/itemIconAppearances.ts:197` maps Coney Foot to a claw; `:205` maps Boar Bristle to hide; `:209` maps Rat Tail to the same horn family as tusks. The contact sheet visibly gives these items the wrong anatomy. `itemIconRenderer.ts:390` uses a constant-radius tube for all horn/tusk/tail variants despite its taper comment. `:230` makes all twelve fish from an ellipsoid and triangular tail/fin. The twelve meat variants are the same smooth ball and bone; hides have one flat cutout shape and patch. Author appropriate silhouette families for foot, bristle, tail, fish body types, tapered horn/tusk/claw, feather barbs, and meat cuts, then apply restrained material detail. Preserve easy raw/cooked/burnt separation and verify every affected variant at 48 px. Rings and pendants also remain simple torus-plus-octahedron models at `:351` and `:359`; finish their setting, chain/band and gem materials. All 16 accessories intentionally have no worn mesh, so this is a declared scope gap if â€œall equipmentâ€ includes visible jewellery on the body.

11. Prevent finished icons from silently staying stale. `tools/generate-item-icons.ts:226` only regenerates missing/invalid files unless `--all` is given; it does not compare geometry, material, appearance or renderer input revisions. After each accepted art change, explicitly regenerate the affected families, or add a content fingerprint so valid old PNGs are invalidated. Keep the existing alpha/margin checks, and add catalogue identity checks for accidental duplicates such as elemental versus plain weapons. Keep duplicate detection advisory for variants intentionally sharing art.

12. Stop converting rare-weapon normal maps to JPEG. `tools/build-minibosses.ts:116` applies a 512 px JPEG pass to retained textures, including normals. Both shipped rare weapons confirm JPEG normal images. Retain normal maps losslessly and give them resolution appropriate to the source detail. This is a focused importer correction; it does not require changing the rare item table or animation contracts.
