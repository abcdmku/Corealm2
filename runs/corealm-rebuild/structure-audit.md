# Structure audit

The structure work is not complete. The checked-out production catalogue contains 16 prefab families, 108 named prefab variants, 19 compositions and three wall-run kits. The four authored settlements place 59 buildings and 16 wall runs. The current material rebuild excludes the medieval building kit, and the structure sweep does not cover every shipped footprint or composition branch. There are also concrete collision defects in the shared composition path.

This report is a read-only source and asset audit requested for the complete-game rebuild. It preserves the existing worktree. It does not accept any feature: no browser session, build, test suite or combined gate was run. Small Node reads enumerated production recipes and assembled collision metadata against the current manifest. Screenshot observations below refer to existing files and are explicitly limited by their age and coverage.

## Material and detail path

The existing source geometry already carries readable roof tiles, wood framing and masonry. Replacing it wholesale before restoring its maps would discard useful work and make fit/collision regressions harder to isolate. The useful first repair is shared by every selected source model.

The exact medieval source archive is available at `C:/Users/Borg/Documents/GitHub/Corealm/.asset-cache/Medieval_Village_MegaKit[Standard].zip`; its SHA-256 matches the pinned source in `tools/build-assets.ts:211`. No download is needed for that repair. Source bindings include real normal and metallic/roughness information, while source AO/emissive bindings are absent for this set. Do not invent an AO pass or claim to restore maps the pack did not author.

Across all 54 village assets, the sources contain 77 normal and 82 metallic/roughness bindings; the shipped assets contain zero of both. Wood, worn wood, rock trim, plaster, brick, uneven brick and round tiles all have authored normal and metallic/roughness maps. Metal ornaments have a roughness map but no normal map. Window glass has scalar factors and no maps. Some files end in `_ORM`, but their source material binds them only as metallic/roughness, not AO.

Restoring bindings alone is insufficient. The image optimization at `tools/build-assets.ts:1607` applies JPEG conversion without a material-slot restriction, and `build-assets.ts:1634` applies 256-color quantization to surviving PNGs. Data maps need a separate resize/encoding policy that preserves their channels and excludes both operations. Preserve scalar factors too: the current roughness floor changes source glass from 0.5 to 0.85. Include the village policy version in the options hash at `build-assets.ts:1750` so the intended assets rebuild while unrelated packs retain their current cache entries.

Runtime architecture flows through `EntityViews.architectureRegion` at `game/src/render/entityViews.ts:3549`, the per-asset role classifier at `game/src/render/materials.ts:276`, and `MaterialLibrary.architecture` at `materials.ts:1298`. Roof, plaster, stone, timber and moss each have regional treatment. Furniture wood is included in this role table; metal and banner cloth retain separate source materials. Preserve that separation when restoring source maps. Preserve shader hooks and material cache identity too, or instanced assets from different regions can collapse into one style.

The shared source importer also serves props/farming and nature. Scope the first import repair to the full medieval selected set. Handle prop materials in a separate explicit round because counters, stalls, doors and farm dressing mix multiple packs. All of those structural consumers still require review before the complete-game claim.
